Usage

Installation

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/fesmc/PlasticIceSheet.jl")

The core package depends only on the standard libraries LinearAlgebra and SparseArrays. Reverse-mode differentiation and NetCDF I/O are loaded on demand through package extensions (below).

Solving

using PlasticIceSheet

z_s, H = solve(z_b, τ_b, mask; dx, dy)
argument meaning
z_b bed elevation z_b (m), nx × ny matrix
τ_b basal shear stress (Pa), scalar or field
mask true where grounded ice exists; the margin is the mask boundary
dx, dy grid spacings (m)

Returns (z_s, H), the surface elevation and thickness. Ice-free cells have H = 0 and z_s = z_b. Pad the mask with at least one ice-free cell, since an ice region without an ice-free cell is unreachable (it is returned with H = 0 and a warning).

Keyword options (see the solve docstring for the rest):

  • mode: :surface (default) solves the plastic surface equation directly over any bed relief, in one sweep solve. :flat solves the flat-bed eikonal for H^2 and drapes it on the bed; it is the cheapest mode and equals :surface on a flat bed. :full adds the bed gradient to the H^2 equation by a damped fixed point (n_outer, outer_tol, relax); it is kept for comparison and does not converge on rough real beds (Algorithms §3).
  • z_ss: sea level (m), scalar or field. Marine margins are grounded at the flotation thickness (\rho_w/\rho_i)\max(z_{ss} - z_b, 0).
  • margin: where the margin lies between an ice cell and its ice-free neighbour. :face (default) puts it on the cell face, :centre on the ice-free cell centre (the behaviour before v0.2), and a field margin_frac ∈ [0, 1) gives its distance from the ice-free cell centre as a fraction of the spacing (Algorithms §6).
  • params: a PlasticParams with the densities ρ_i, ρ_w, gravity g and the thickness floor H_min.

A margin known as a signed distance (m, positive in the ice) converts to margin_frac:

frac = margin_frac_from_sdist(sdist, mask, dx, dy)
z_s, H = solve(z_b, τ_b, mask; dx, dy, margin = frac)

On a 300 × 300 grid a :surface solve takes about 6 ms, :flat about 3 ms and a converging :full solve about 1 s.

Diagnostics

ice_volume(H, dx, dy)                          # m³
ice_area(mask, dx, dy)                         # m²
surface_misfit(z_s, z_s_obs, mask)             # mean-squared surface misfit over ice
r = stress_residual(z_s, z_b, τ_b, mask; dx, dy)   # |ρ_i g H |∇z_s| − τ_b| / τ_b per cell

stress_residual checks how well a geometry satisfies the plastic stress balance, in any mode. It is a cell-centred measure and is of order 1 at the first ice cell (the one-sided slope of a square-root profile), so read it away from the margin. On a 300 m bed bump the median over the interior is about 0.04 for :surface and 0.5 for :flat.

Velocity and surface mass balance

The plastic solve returns only geometry. A DIVA closure on the fixed geometry recovers a depth-averaged velocity and a first-order SMB (Algorithms §8). Pick a rheology and a sliding law:

rheology = GlenRheology(A = 1.0e-16, n = 3.0)            # isothermal Glen, per year
sliding  = WeertmanSliding(β = 1.0e4, m = 3.0)           # or LinearSliding(β),
                                                         # RegularizedCoulomb(C, u_0, m; u_max)

The coefficient (β, or C for RegularizedCoulomb) may be a field of the grid’s shape. RegularizedCoulomb returns a NaN sliding speed where τ_b ≥ C, since the bed is at its Coulomb cap and the speed is undetermined. With a finite speed cap u_max, the speed is min(u_b, u_max) instead, and u_max at the cap.

Closure B: velocity, then implied SMB

vel = diva_velocity(z_s, H, τ_b, mask, dx, dy; rheology, sliding)
# named tuple: ux, uy, speed, u_b (sliding), u_def (deformation), ex, ey (flow direction)

smb = smb_from_velocity(vel, H, z_s, mask, dx, dy)      # ḃ = ∇·(ūH), MFD operator

Closure A: balance velocity from a prescribed SMB

bal = balance_velocity(z_s, H, mask, smb, dx, dy)       # named tuple: speed, ux, uy, Q, ...
ib  = implied_basal_velocity(z_s, H, τ_b, mask, smb, dx, dy; rheology)
# ib.u_basal = u_balance − u_def: the basal velocity that geometry and SMB demand

balance_flux is the exact inverse of smb_from_velocity, so routing closure B’s SMB back through closure A recovers closure B’s flux to machine precision.

SMB-driven reconstruction

The dual of solve drives the geometry with an SMB (m ice yr⁻¹) instead of a yield stress, on the same fixed margin (Algorithms §9):

z_s, H = diva_reconstruct(z_b, mask, smb, dx, dy; rheology, sliding, z_ss)

The default method = :steady solves steady mass conservation \nabla\cdot\mathbf q = \dot b with H \ge 0 directly. Mask cells where ablation exceeds the inflow end ice-free. It stops when the residual falls below res_tol (m yr⁻¹) and warns otherwise. On Greenland at 16 km (examples/reconstruct_greenland.jl) it takes a few seconds.

The implied SMB of this method for a given geometry, with the margin outflow and the surface velocity of the same closure, is:

o = smb_flux_divergence(z_b, H, mask, dx, dy; rheology, sliding, z_ss, τ_cap = τ_b)
# named tuple: smb, q_land, q_marine (outflow per margin cell), us_x, us_y

The geometry H is a root of the steady problem forced by o.smb. τ_cap (optional) caps the driving stress of the fluxes, since a plastic bed carries at most its yield stress.

method = :routing keeps the earlier iteration (route the SMB, invert the flux law for the driving stress, plastic sweep, under-relax). It is stable only for relax below roughly slope × Δx / H, i.e., 0.003–0.04 in tests, and is kept for comparison.

Inverting for \tau_b

The solver is differentiable with respect to \tau_b (Algorithms §7).

Forward mode: scalar or low-dimensional \tau_b

using ForwardDiff
loss(τ_b) = surface_misfit(first(solve(z_b, τ_b, mask; dx, dy)), z_s_obs, mask)
g = ForwardDiff.derivative(loss, 1.0e5)

Reverse mode: a full per-cell \tau_b field

Load the extension triggers, then differentiate differentiable_thickness with any reverse-mode AD:

using ImplicitDifferentiation, ADTypes, ForwardDiff, Zygote

loss(τ_b) = surface_misfit(differentiable_thickness(τ_b, z_b, mask; dx, dy) .+ z_b, z_s_obs, mask)
g = Zygote.gradient(loss, τ_b)[1]      # gradient with respect to the whole τ_b field

differentiable_thickness takes the same mode, z_ss and margin keywords as solve. The default reverse-mode linear solver is direct, which is robust for modest grids. For very large fields, pass an iterative solver (see the differentiable_thickness docstring).

examples/invert_tau.jl recovers a spatially varying \tau_b from an observed surface with Adam, and Inversion covers the parameterizations of \tau_b on real data.

NetCDF I/O

using NCDatasets

inp = load_plastic_inputs("inputs.nc")             # (; z_b, τ_b, mask, dx, dy, x, y)
z_s, H = solve(inp.z_b, inp.τ_b, inp.mask; inp.dx, inp.dy)
save_reconstruction("out.nc", z_s, H; x = inp.x, y = inp.y)

Examples

The scripts in examples/ run with julia --project=examples examples/<script>.jl. The Greenland scripts read the GRL-16KM or GRL-32KM grids from an examples/ice_data directory (not included).

script content
invert_tau.jl synthetic per-cell \tau_b recovery with Adam (reverse mode)
invert_taub_greenland.jl \tau_b inversion on Greenland, tiled or per-cell (METHOD)
invert_taub_sweep_greenland.jl rmse against the number of tiles
smb_from_velocity_greenland.jl implied SMB from observed velocity and thickness, against the climate SMB
reconstruct_greenland.jl SMB-driven reconstruction from the MAR SMB, with balance velocity

Caveats

  • The geometry is steady state only. It is biased thick where the real ice was streaming or otherwise dynamic, since the plastic assumption sees no reduced driving stress there.
  • \tau_b carries essentially all of the uncertainty. For a first guess a constant (about 50–100 kPa) or a bed-keyed value is reasonable, but the result is not a constrained reconstruction.
  • :surface and :flat are first-order accurate. At 10–30 km the margin position and the coarse inputs change the ice volume by several percent (Algorithms §6).

Fortran library

PlasticIceSheet is a dependency-free Fortran port of the forward solver and the closures (libplastic.a), for coupling into Fortran models. It uses the same schemes (results were verified bit-identical against v0.1) and adds a warm start, OpenMP parallelism and a joint inversion of speed and SMB. The \tau_b inversion and the AD extension remain Julia-only.
