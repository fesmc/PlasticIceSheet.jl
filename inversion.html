Inverting basal shear stress

The plastic solve turns a margin, a bed and a basal shear stress \tau_b into an ice sheet. \tau_b carries essentially all of the freedom of the reconstruction, so the practical question is the inverse one: which \tau_b makes the reconstruction match the data? This page describes how the inverse problem is posed, the two parameterizations of \tau_b in the examples, and how the tiled parameterization automates the hand-tuned shear-stress domains of ICESHEET (Gowan et al., 2016, 2021). The gradient itself (implicit differentiation of the converged solve) is described in Algorithms §7.

The inverse problem

We minimize an objective on the reconstruction over \tau_b. For present-day ice the natural target is the observed surface, through surface_misfit:

\min_{\tau_b}\; \big\langle\, (z_s(\tau_b) - z_s^{\text{obs}})^2 \,\big\rangle_{\text{mask}}, \qquad z_s(\tau_b) = z_b + H(\tau_b).

differentiable_thickness gives the gradient by reverse-mode AD, and the examples minimize the misfit with Adam. The grounded mask is the prescribed margin, an input of ICESHEET that is not inverted. The recovered ice extent therefore always equals the mask, and only the geometry inside it is fit.

The number of free parameters in \tau_b should match what the target data can resolve. A dense present-day surface constrains a dense field, while a sparse paleo target (relative sea level) constrains only a few regional values. The two parameterizations below sit at these two ends.

Two parameterizations

The example examples/invert_taub_greenland.jl implements both and switches between them with the environment variable METHOD:

julia --project=examples examples/invert_taub_greenland.jl                  # METHOD=tiled (default)
METHOD=percell julia --project=examples examples/invert_taub_greenland.jl   # per cell

The objective, the Adam loop and the outputs are shared. Only the map from the optimization parameters to the \tau_b field differs. All results below are for Greenland at 32 km (GRL-32KM, about 2000 ice cells), mode = :surface and margin = :face.

Per cell (METHOD=percell)

A full per-cell \tau_b field is regularized to a length scale REG_KM by a Gaussian-blur reparameterization. We optimize a raw field \varphi and set

\tau_b = \operatorname{clamp}\big(K_x\,\varphi\,K_y\big),

where K_x and K_y are separable 1-D Gaussian smoothers with \sigma = \texttt{REG\_KM}/\Delta x cells. The blur is linear, so it differentiates cleanly under Zygote, and it ensures that \tau_b varies only on scales of at least REG_KM regardless of the data fit. This parameterization has many effective degrees of freedom and fits a dense observed surface closely (about 44 m rmse with REG_KM = 32 km).

Tiled (METHOD=tiled, default)

The tiled parameterization is low-dimensional and transferable to paleo ice sheets. Cells are grouped into tiles defined only from data available for an unknown ice sheet: bed roughness (the windowed standard deviation of z_b, binned into N_ROUGH quantile classes) crossed with a sediment mask (sediment thickness split into soft and hard at SED_THRESH). The ice thickness and ice-derived drainage basins are never used to draw the tiles. Each tile d carries one value \theta_d:

\tau_b(\mathbf x) = \operatorname{clamp}\Big(\textstyle\sum_d \theta_d\, \mathbb 1[\,\mathbf x \in d\,]\Big),

embedded by a one-hot selector matrix, which is linear and thus differentiable under Zygote. The default is N_ROUGH = 4 × 2 sediment classes, i.e., 8 tiles, which reach about 220 m rmse. The observed surface enters only as the calibration target. This is the modern-surface calibration Gowan et al. (2021) used for Greenland and Antarctica, and the place a relative-sea-level objective would take in a paleo reconstruction.

Relation to ICESHEET

ICESHEET (Gowan et al., 2016) is the same perfectly plastic, equilibrium forward model. In PaleoMIST (Gowan et al., 2021), \tau_b was chosen by three steps. First, the target was relative sea level through a GIA model for the paleo ice sheets, plus geological margins, and the modern surface for Greenland and Antarctica. Second, the shear-stress domains were defined by topographic variability (mountainous or flat) and refined by surficial geology (continuous sediment for low \tau_b, hard or discontinuous cover for high \tau_b). Third, the domain values were tuned by hand until the fit was acceptable.

The tiled method follows this logic but automates both halves: the domains come from bed roughness and sediment, and the domain values come from gradient descent. On Greenland it recovers the premise of Gowan et al.: the soft-sediment tiles settle at about 5–46 kPa and the hard-bed tiles at about 51–99 kPa.

How many tiles? A resolution test

The number of domains, which ICESHEET left to judgement, can be chosen quantitatively. examples/invert_taub_sweep_greenland.jl sweeps the tile count (100 Adam iterations each) and plots the rmse against the number of free parameters. The knee of that curve is the resolution the data support:

roughness classes tiles, roughness only rmse (m) tiles, with sediment split rmse (m)
1 1 319 2 250
2 2 283 4 237
3 3 269 6 213
4 4 273 8 220
6 6 268 11 212
8 8 264 15 210

Sediment controls the fit more than roughness does. A single hard/soft split (2 tiles) reduces the rmse from about 320 to 250 m, more than eight roughness-only tiles achieve (about 264 m). The knee lies at about 6 tiles, beyond which further parameters change the rmse by less than 5 m. Even the best tiling (about 210 m) remains well above the per-cell fit (about 44 m). Thus, a substantial part of the surface signal reflects variations of \tau_b that static bed roughness and sediment do not explain, most likely the basal thermal and hydrological state.

Conditioning: choice of mode

The inversions use mode = :surface, the default. Its scheme is monotone (Algorithms §2), so the residual Jacobian of the adjoint is well conditioned, as for :flat. Including the bed relief, however, improves the fit: with the default 8 tiles :surface reaches about 220 m rmse and :flat about 312 m, at a similar cost (about 4–5 min for 250 iterations). :full is not suitable, since its damped fixed point does not converge on real beds, and its dense adjoint system becomes singular on large grids. For finer grids the cost of the per-cell gradient is dominated by the direct linear solve. See the differentiable_thickness docstring for the iterative-solver option.

Toward a paleo (RSL) reconstruction

The tiled parameterization links to a paleo reconstruction: keep the bed-derived tiles and replace the observed-surface objective by relative sea level computed with a GIA model. The tiles need no knowledge of the ice sheet, so the same setup transfers to an ice sheet whose geometry is unknown. That is the regime ICESHEET was built for, and the one in which reducing the number of parameters is required rather than optional.
